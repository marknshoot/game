RELAY

A 3-player asymmetric co-op parkour game. One player runs a level they can barely read. One player controls the level but cannot speak and cannot see the runner. One player is the only voice in the building — and cannot hear anything at all.

Playable prototype: one level, three private screens, the whole chain of communication enforced by the game.

▶ Play the 1-person demo (all three panels) Runner Operator Bridge

60-second guided demo (solo view)

1. Open the 1-person demo. Three private panels appear — nobody can see anybody else's.
2. In the Runner panel press P (autopilot, a demo helper so one person can drive everything).
3. Watch the Bridge panel: GAP (A3) — 5.7 m / 0.86 s ◀ CALL NOW. When it flashes CALL NOW, press T in the Bridge panel ("THROW") and immediately press B in the Operator panel. The slide bridge must be out before the Runner reaches the edge.
4. The bridge motor only stays out for 3 seconds and locks after two throws inside 8 seconds, so a mistimed throw is a real mistake.
5. At PHANTOM FLOOR (D4), nothing looks different to the Runner — the floor is a lie. When the countdown hits ~4.5 m, press 3 ("JUMP NOW") in the Bridge panel and watch the Runner trust you. He falls if you are late.
6. At TWO-KEY EXIT (F4): E in the Operator panel arms it, then hold K in the Bridge panel for 1.5 s. Neither screen can finish the level alone.
7. Now try the other two panels with the mouse only, and notice what you cannot do from each one.

The whole idea in one beat

There is a gap in the floor. The Runner can see a hole but cannot see whether the slide-bridge is out — and at a full sprint they physically cannot stop once they are within 2.5 m of the edge. The Operator has the lever that throws the bridge, but the bridge takes 0.7 s to slide out, and the Operator cannot see where the Runner is. Only the Bridge sees the run, and only the Bridge can talk. 

span travel 0.7 s  +  operator reaction 0.25 s  =  0.95 s
runner sprint 6.6 m/s  ->  the Bridge has to speak about 2 seconds early.

That number is the game. Every level is built so that the information expires: a call that was correct when it was spoken is lethal by the time it lands. The other two beats in the prototype are a phantom floor (visually identical to concrete, a hole in the collision — only the Operator's board shows it) and a two-key exit (the Operator arms it, the Bridge physically holds the second key).

Why they can't just talk to each other

RUNNER — BLIND

Has: parkour, reflexes, touch. Speaks out loud.

Missing: labels, colours, door states, the map. Sees only a few metres of raw shape.

Hears: the Bridge. Nobody else answers.

Their callouts go to the Operator's ear only — the Operator can hear them and cannot reply.

OPERATOR — MUTE

Has: the full board — door states, timings, hazards — and every lever.

Missing: a voice, and any view of the Runner. No live feed of them at all.

Hears: the Bridge and the Runner.

Their language is stamps, knocks, and the world itself. A door opening is the word "go".

BRIDGE — DEAF

Has: the Runner's cam, the Operator's board, the only voice, the chalk, the second key.

Missing: all audio — no footsteps, no door hiss, no voice from anyone.

Job: fuse two lossy pictures into instructions that arrive before the commitment point.

Chalk is a limited resource: 6 arrows. The Runner can carry at most what you draw.

Controls

ScreenKeysMouse
Runner A/D or arrows to run, SPACE to jump, P autopilot (demo helper) —
Operator B throw the slide bridge, E exit lever, N knock, 7/8/9 pick a stamp Click the board to stamp a location at the current grid point
Bridge 1 GO, 2 WAIT, 3 JUMP NOW, 4 STOP, 5 LEFT, 6 RIGHT, T THROW, O ON ONE, K hold the 2nd key, C cycle chalk, V quick chalk Click the Runner's cam to draw a chalk arrow there

Playing with three people

Open the three Runner / Operator / Bridge links above in three windows (or three machines on the same network if you serve the folder). The tabs find each other automatically — the Runner's tab owns the simulation, and everyone else syncs to it. There is nothing to configure.

Important for a real test: play from separate rooms with headphones, or at least agree that only the Bridge may speak and the Operator only uses stamps and the world. Three people crowding one laptop will just talk to each other and the game disappears. The 1-person demo exists so you can see the machinery on your own.

What the prototype is honestly testing

  • Does a timed relay feel different from a puzzle relay? (Bombanana, Keep Talking) — the plan is only interesting if the plan expires.
  • Is the Bridge busy in a fun way, or just a telephone wire? (Watch whether the Bridge has to choose what to say.)
  • Is the Operator's mute-ness painful and funny, or just annoying? (Watch for emergent language: two-door-flickers = "wait".)
  • Does the Runner feel blind-but-capable, and is the blame funny instead of toxic? (Instant respawn, ghost replay, no per-player death stats.)
