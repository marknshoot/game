RELAY 3D · LAN

Three players. One blind Runner who can only see thirteen metres of shape. One mute Operator who owns every lever and cannot see the Runner at all. One deaf Bridge who is the only voice on the team and hears nothing.

A 3D parkour relay across a gantry: a retractable span, a floor that is a lie, a lift only the Operator can move, and an exit that needs two keys from two different laptops.

RUNNER BLIND

WASD + mouse, SPACE to jump. Thirteen metres of fog, no labels, no timers, no map. You will run off things that are not there.

Your voice reaches the Operator. Nobody else can hear you.

OPERATOR MUTE

The whole board: span, lift, exit, hazard marks, timings. Every lever. No feed of the Runner — not even a dot.

Stamps and KNOCKs are your voice. Lever timing is your handwriting.

BRIDGE DEAF

You see his cam and the Operator's board, you are the only one who can speak, you hold the second key and six chalk marks. You hear nothing at all.

Fuse two lossy pictures into words that arrive before he commits.

Play it on three laptops

  1. On one laptop (any of the three): cd game && node server.js.
  2. It prints a LAN address, e.g. http://192.168.1.24:8791/. Open that address on all three laptops.
  3. Use the same room code, pick a different role on each laptop. Each role can only be held by one machine.
  4. Keep the windows visible (browsers throttle hidden ones).

If a role is missing the game still runs — you just have to do without them. Two players in a room is a legitimate (and much harder) way to play.

The one number the game is built on

bridge speaks → 0.25 s reaction → 0.7 s mechanism  ≈  1 second
at 6.6 m/s that is 6.6 metres of runway the Bridge has to spend before the world moves.

So the Bridge's job is not to describe the world, it is to schedule it. Measured windows for this level (from the headless harness, not from vibes): span throw z ∈ [4, 15], phantom-floor jump z ∈ [41, 44.5], call window d ∈ [1.5, 5] m before the hole.
